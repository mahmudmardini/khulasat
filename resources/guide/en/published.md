# The published page, as readers see it {#published}

The published page is what you share with people. Its readers need no account and no sign-up, and it works on any phone or computer.

![The top of the published page: the title, the key verse, the gathering card, and the language buttons above.](shot:published-page)

## What is on the page {#published-parts}

- **Language buttons** at the top, if the summary is published in more than one language.
- **The header**: your institution's logo, the lecture title and subtitle, and the key verse in the Mushaf script with the surah name and verse number.
- **The gathering card**: the speaker, the place, the Hijri and Gregorian dates and the time of delivery, according to what you entered and the attribution style you chose.
- **The lecture's sections**: each with a heading and its paragraphs. Verses appear in the Mushaf wording, hadiths with their references and gradings, along with comparison tables and practical steps.
- **Buttons at the end of the text**: **Share the summary**, **Save as PDF**, and **Test your understanding** if the summary has an open comprehension quiz.
- **References for the verses and hadiths**: a list at the end of the page with the location of every verse and the reference and grading of every hadith.
- **The footer**: your institution's disclaimer, a line saying the summary was prepared by an AI tool and that its verses and hadiths were checked against their sources by text matching, and a link to **report an error in this summary**.

![The end of the page: the gathering card, the references, the disclaimer and the "report an error" link.](shot:published-footer)

## On a phone {#published-mobile}

The page is designed for phones first: the text is readable, the verses are in the Mushaf script, and readers do not need to zoom.

![The published page on a phone screen.](shot:published-mobile)

## The page in another language {#published-locales}

On translated pages:

- The text and its headings are translated.
- **A verse** keeps its Arabic text, with **an approved translation of its meaning** below it, published and with a known source, not an AI translation.
- **A hadith** keeps its Arabic wording, and the text below it is labelled "translation of the meaning", so nobody takes it for the Prophet's ﷺ own words in another language.

## Sharing {#published-share}

When you paste the page's link into WhatsApp, Telegram or similar apps, a **preview card** appears with it, showing the lecture title and your institution's name.

## Reporting an error {#published-complaint}

At the foot of every page is a link to **report an error in this summary**. It opens a form that needs no account, where the reader chooses the type of objection and writes the page link, a way to reach them, and the details.

![The "Report an error" form.](shot:complaint)

| Type of objection | Stated response time |
|---|---|
| An error in a citation's reference or grading | {{evidence_complaint_days}} days |
| An objection to how words were attributed, or to the summary | {{takedown_hours}} hours |
| A removal request | {{takedown_hours}} hours |
| Something else | {{evidence_complaint_days}} days |

Complaints go to the **platform administrator**, not directly to your institution, and they decide: a correction, removing the page, or no action. If your page is removed, the system tells you on the summary screen, as in [If your page is removed after a complaint](#preview-takedown).

> [!LIMIT]
> - There are no comments on the page, and readers cannot edit it.
> - The system does not know who read the page: no names, no emails, no cookies.
